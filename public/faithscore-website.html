Why FaithScore exists

Tonic sol-fa never left. Most notation software just stopped supporting it.

Across choirs in African and Commonwealth churches, tonic sol-fa is still how most singers read music — a system of movable syllables, not lines and dots. It's how generations learned to sing in parts, and it's still printed in hymn books every week.

Mainstream notation software was built around the stave. If your choir reads sol-fa, you're on your own — copying a piece out by hand, syllable by syllable, every time something needs to change.

FaithScore keeps both notations as first-class citizens of the same piece. Write in whichever one you think in, convert to the other when you need to, and keep every lyric, tie, slur, and chord intact along the way.
